Weighted vs Unweighted GPA: How to Calculate Each

📅 October 19, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ ToolPond

The difference, the formulas and a worked example you can follow

An unweighted GPA scores every class on the same 4.0 scale, so an A is always 4.0. A weighted GPA adds extra points for harder classes, usually +0.5 for honors and +1.0 for AP or IB, so an A in AP can count as 5.0. To calculate either one, multiply each grade's points by its credits, add the results, and divide by the total credits. Our free GPA calculator does both: the 4.0 scale gives an unweighted GPA, and the 5.0 scale gives a weighted one.

Quick answer

Unweighted GPA = total grade points ÷ total credits, with A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0. Weighted GPA uses the same formula, but each grade gets a bonus for honors (+0.5) or AP/IB (+1.0). The exact bonus is set by your school, so check your school's policy.

The two numbers describe the same report card. A student with all A's in regular classes has a 4.0 unweighted and a 4.0 weighted GPA. A student with all A's in AP classes has a 4.0 unweighted and a 5.0 weighted GPA.

With plus and minus grades, a common unweighted scale is A+ and A = 4.0, A- = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B- = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C- = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, D- = 0.7, F = 0. Many schools do not give bonus points for an F, so a failed AP class still counts as 0.

How to calculate an unweighted GPA

1

Convert each grade to points

A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0, ignoring whether the class is regular, honors or AP.

2

Multiply by credits

If every class is worth the same credit, you can skip this step and simply average the points.

3

Add and divide

Unweighted GPA = total grade points ÷ total credits.

How to calculate a weighted GPA

The formula is the same. The only change is step 1: add the course bonus to the grade points before you multiply.

Formula

Weighted GPA = Σ (grade points + course bonus) × credits ÷ Σ credits

Example: a B (3.0) in an AP course with a +1.0 bonus counts as 4.0. An A (4.0) in an honors course with a +0.5 bonus counts as 4.5.

Credit hours and cumulative GPA

When classes carry different credits, as in college, multiply each grade's points by its credits first. A 4-credit class then counts more than a 2-credit one.

CourseGradePointsCreditsPoints × credits
BiologyA4.0312.0
CalculusB3.0412.0
EconomicsC2.036.0
WritingA4.028.0
Total1238.0

GPA = 38.0 ÷ 12 = 3.17.

To combine semesters into a cumulative GPA, do not average the two GPAs. Weight each by its credits. If semester one is 15 credits at 3.40 and semester two is 16 credits at 3.80, the cumulative GPA is (15 × 3.40 + 16 × 3.80) ÷ 31 = 111.8 ÷ 31 = 3.61. A simple average would give 3.60, which is slightly off.

Which GPA do colleges use?

It depends on the college. Some read the GPA printed on your transcript, some recalculate their own, and many look at the course list to see how hard your classes were. A weighted GPA shows you took demanding classes, while an unweighted GPA lets an admissions office compare students from schools with different weighting rules. Because policies differ, check the admissions page of each college you are applying to. For scholarships and honor rolls, follow the definition in the rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?
An unweighted GPA treats every class equally on a 4.0 scale. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder classes, usually +0.5 for honors and +1.0 for AP or IB, so it can be above 4.0.
How do I calculate a weighted GPA?
Add the course bonus to each grade's points, multiply by the credits, add everything up and divide by total credits. For example, a B in AP with a +1.0 bonus counts as 4.0.
How do I calculate an unweighted GPA?
Convert each grade to points (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0), multiply by credits, add the results and divide by the total number of credits.
Is a weighted GPA higher than an unweighted GPA?
Usually yes, if you take honors, AP or IB classes. If every class is regular, the two are identical.
Can a weighted GPA be higher than 4.0?
Yes. On a 5.0 weighted scale, a student with straight A's in AP classes has a 5.0. The maximum depends on your school's scale.
What is a good GPA?
A 3.0 is a common minimum for many scholarships and programs, 3.5 and above is considered strong, and 4.0 is the unweighted maximum. What counts as good depends on your goals and your school.
Do colleges prefer weighted or unweighted GPA?
Policies differ. Some colleges recalculate GPA themselves, and many consider the difficulty of your courses as well. Check the admissions page of each college.

Conclusion

Weighted and unweighted GPA use the same formula, grade points times credits divided by total credits, and differ only in the bonus for harder classes. To check your own numbers, enter your courses in the GPA calculator.

This guide is general information. Grade scales, bonus points and GPA rules differ by school and college, so follow your own school's policy.
