How to Write the Amount in Words on a Cheque

November 5, 2026 · 7 min read · By ToolPond

Step-by-step rules, examples for US dollars, pounds, Indian and Pakistani rupees, and the mistakes that get cheques returned

Write the amount in words starting at the far left of the line, add the currency and the cents or paise, then close the line with a line or the word “only” so nothing can be added. The words must match the figures in the amount box. If they differ, the bank may go by the words or return the cheque.

Quick answer

Where the words go on a cheque

A cheque (also spelled check in the US) has two places for the amount, and both must say the same thing:

  • The amount box: the figures, such as 5,075.62, usually at the right.
  • The words line: the same amount spelled out, under the payee line. This line is the one banks treat with the most care, because it is harder to alter than numbers.

The payee line (“Pay to the order of”) names who is paid. This guide is about the words line.

How to write the amount in words, step by step

  1. Start at the far left of the words line, so no one can add words in front.
  2. Write the whole-number part in words. For 5,075 write “Five thousand seventy-five”. Hyphenate numbers from 21 to 99, such as seventy-five.
  3. Add the currency name: dollars, pounds, rupees.
  4. Write the cents or paise. Use words (“and sixty-two cents”) or, in the US, a fraction (“and 62/100”).
  5. Close the line. Add “only” or draw a line through the unused space up to the word “Dollars” or the end of the line.
  6. Compare with the figures in the amount box. Every digit must agree.
  7. Sign and date the cheque, then check once more before handing it over.

Lakh and crore: a lakh is 100,000 and a crore is 10,000,000. So 1,00,000 is “one lakh” and 1,50,00,000 is “one crore fifty lakh”. The converter's numbering box switches between the Indian system and the international one.

When the words and figures differ

Banks usually treat the amount in words as the amount to pay when the two differ. This is the rule in many places, including under the Negotiable Instruments Act in India and Pakistan and under US commercial law. In practice some banks refuse to process a cheque with a mismatch and return it, which delays payment. The safest approach is to make them match every time, and to avoid corrections. If you make a mistake, write a new cheque instead of overwriting.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving a gap after the words, where extra words or digits could be added.
  • Mixing up the figures and the words, such as 15,000 in the box and “fifty thousand” on the line.
  • Forgetting the cents or paise when the amount has decimals.
  • Using the wrong numbering system. 1,00,000 is “one lakh”, not “one hundred thousand”, if your bank uses lakh and crore.
  • Writing the numbers in the wrong order, such as “twenty hundred” for 2,000.
  • Overwriting or crossing out instead of starting a fresh cheque.

Frequently asked questions

How do you write the amount in words on a cheque?

Start at the far left of the line, write the whole amount in words, add the currency name, write the cents or paise as words or as a fraction, and close the line with a line or the word only. The words must match the figures in the amount box.

How do you write cents on a cheque?

In the US, write the cents as a fraction over 100, such as Five thousand seventy-five and 62/100 dollars, or as words, such as and sixty-two cents. If there are no cents, write 00/100 or add only. Use the style your bank expects.

Do you write only on a cheque?

In India and Pakistan it is common to end the words with only so nothing can be added. In the US and UK many people draw a line through the empty space instead. Both achieve the same goal: no gap for changes.

How do you write 1 lakh in words on a cheque?

Write one lakh rupees only for 1,00,000. For 1,25,000 write one lakh twenty-five thousand rupees only. The Indian system uses lakh (100,000) and crore (10,000,000) instead of hundred thousand and million.

What if the words and figures on a cheque do not match?

Generally the amount in words is treated as the amount to pay, for example under the Negotiable Instruments Act in India and Pakistan and under US commercial law. Some banks return the cheque instead. Always make them match.

Should I use and in a pounds amount?

British English usually writes one hundred and five pounds, with and after the hundreds. American English usually leaves it out. Follow your bank's style.

Can I use the converter to fill in a cheque?

Yes. The converter writes the standard wording and can add only at the end. You are responsible for checking the result against your bank's rules before you sign.

How do I write a cheque amount with decimals in words?

Write the whole part in words with the currency, then and, then the decimals as the minor unit. For example 125,000.50 rupees is one lakh twenty-five thousand rupees and fifty paisas only.
