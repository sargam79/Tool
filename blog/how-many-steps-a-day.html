How Many Steps a Day Do You Actually Need?

📅 October 26, 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read ✍️ ToolPond

Evidence-based step goals by age and goal, and what they mean in miles, minutes and calories

For most adults, about 7,000 to 8,000 steps a day captures most of the health benefit, and more is better up to roughly 10,000 for weight loss and fitness. The famous 10,000-step figure is a round number, not a medical threshold. Older adults often reach the benefit plateau earlier, at around 6,000 to 8,000 steps.

Quick answer

Aim for 7,000 to 8,000 steps a day for general health, 8,000 to 10,000 if you are under 60 or want weight-loss support, and 6,000 to 8,000 if you are 60 or older. For a 5 ft 8 in (173 cm) person, 10,000 steps is about 4.4 miles (7.1 km). Your own step length changes the distance, so convert your steps with the stride calculator.

What the research says about daily steps

Large studies that follow people for years show the same pattern: risk of early death falls quickly as you move from very low step counts to moderate ones, and then the gains slow down.

  • 2025 pooled analysis (The Lancet Public Health): combining 57 studies and more than 160,000 adults, people walking about 7,000 steps a day had roughly 47% lower risk of death from any cause than people walking about 2,000. The same level was linked to lower risk of cardiovascular disease (about 25%), dementia (about 38%), depression (about 22%) and type 2 diabetes (about 14%).
  • 2022 meta-analysis (The Lancet Public Health): the benefit leveled off at about 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day for adults aged 60 and over, and at about 8,000 to 10,000 for adults under 60.
  • 2020 US study (JAMA): adults taking 8,000 steps a day had about 51% lower risk of death over the follow-up period than those taking 4,000, and those taking 12,000 had about 65% lower risk.

Where did 10,000 come from? It traces back to a 1960s Japanese pedometer marketed under a name that roughly means "10,000-step meter". It was a catchy number, and later research happened to find that it sits at the generous end of a useful range.

Read these numbers with care

These are observational studies. People who walk more also tend to be healthier in other ways, so steps are linked to better outcomes but are not proven to cause every one of them. The figures above are averages for groups, not promises for one person.

The most useful rule is simple: whatever you do now, add a little. The biggest health gains come from lifting yourself out of the lowest step counts, not from chasing a perfect number.

Activity levels by daily steps

Researchers who study pedometer data often use these bands to describe how active a person is:

12,500 or moreHighly active

Find your band by wearing a phone or watch for a normal week and averaging the days. Count weekends too, since many people walk much less on days off.

What your steps equal in miles and km

Distance depends on your step length. A common estimate for walking is step length = height × 0.414, and a mile is 63,360 inches. Using that estimate:

5 ft 4 in (163 cm)26.5 in2.09 mi / 3.4 km2.93 mi / 4.7 km4.18 mi / 6.7 km

So the answer to "how many miles is 10,000 steps" is roughly 4 to 5 miles for most adults, shorter for shorter people. For your exact figure, see the steps-in-a-mile guide, and for measuring your own stride see the stride length guide.

How long does 10,000 steps take?

At a steady walking pace of 3.5 mph, a 5 ft 8 in person covers 4.44 miles in about 76 minutes, and 7,000 steps (3.11 miles) takes about 53 minutes. You rarely do it in one go: a typical day already contains several thousand steps from moving around the home and workplace, so the walk you add on purpose is shorter than it sounds.

Pace also matters. A cadence of about 100 steps per minute or more is generally treated as moderate intensity, the level health guidelines count toward the weekly 150 minutes of moderate activity. Slower strolling still counts toward your total but is gentler.

Steps and weight loss

Walking burns roughly 0.5 to 1 calorie per pound of body weight per mile, so a 70 kg (154 lb) person walking 10,000 steps (about 4.4 miles) burns roughly 290 to 380 calories, depending on speed and whether you count only the extra calories above resting. Using the common 3,500 calories per pound approximation, that is about one pound every 9 to 12 days if nothing else changes.

  • Steps help most when paired with a modest calorie target. See how big a calorie deficit should be.
  • Walking more can raise appetite for some people, so check your weekly weight trend instead of assuming a number.
  • Heavier people burn more per step, because they move more mass.

How to add steps without a gym

  • Build from your average: add 500 to 1,000 steps a day each week until you reach your target.
  • Walk after meals: a 10 to 15 minute walk adds roughly 1,000 to 1,800 steps.
  • Take calls on your feet: pacing during a 20 minute call can add 1,500 or more.
  • Park farther away or get off one stop early: small, repeatable additions are easier to keep.
  • Use stairs when you can: they also raise effort per step.
  • Walk with someone: a fixed time with a friend or colleague makes it a habit.

Stay hydrated on longer walks. If you are not sure how much to drink, the water intake calculator gives a daily estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many steps a day should I take?
For most adults, 7,000 to 8,000 steps a day captures much of the health benefit. Adults under 60 often benefit up to about 8,000 to 10,000, and adults 60 and over up to about 6,000 to 8,000. If you walk very little now, add steps gradually.
Is 10,000 steps a day necessary?
No. The 10,000 figure is a round target that came from a pedometer marketing name. Studies find most of the benefit by about 7,000 to 8,000 steps, though 10,000 is a reasonable goal for fitness and weight loss.
How many miles is 10,000 steps?
About 4 to 5 miles (6.3 to 7.6 km) for most adults. A 5 ft 8 in person covers roughly 4.4 miles (7.1 km), because step length is about 0.414 times height.
How many steps is 5 miles?
About 11,300 steps for a 5 ft 8 in person (2,251 steps per mile × 5). Shorter people need more steps and taller people fewer.
Are 7,000 steps a day enough?
For general health, 7,000 steps is linked in large studies to a much lower risk of early death compared with about 2,000 steps. For weight loss or higher fitness, you may want more, together with a sensible calorie target.
How many calories does 10,000 steps burn?
Roughly 290 to 380 calories for a 70 kg (154 lb) person who walks at about 3.5 mph. The number rises with body weight and pace.
Do steps count if I walk slowly?
Yes. Every step adds to your daily total. Faster walking, about 100 steps per minute or more, counts as moderate exercise and burns more per minute.

Conclusion

For most adults the sweet spot is about 7,000 to 8,000 steps a day, with 8,000 to 10,000 a good aim if you are under 60 or want weight-loss support. Start from your current average, add a little each week, and use the stride calculator to turn steps into miles or kilometers for your height.

This guide is for general information and is not medical advice. Research figures describe averages across groups. Talk to your doctor before making big changes to your activity, especially if you have a health condition.
