Calorie Deficit: How Many Calories Should You Cut?

📅 October 26, 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read ✍️ ToolPond

A safe deficit size, the weekly loss it gives, and worked examples you can copy

A calorie deficit of 250 to 500 calories below your maintenance level is the usual starting point, and it leads to roughly 0.5 to 1 pound (0.25 to 0.5 kg) of weight loss a week. Your maintenance level is your TDEE, the calories you burn in a day. Bigger deficits lose weight faster but are harder to keep up and can cost muscle.

Quick answer

Daily calories to lose weight = TDEE − deficit. If your TDEE is 2,200, eating about 1,700 calories gives a 500 calorie deficit, or about 1 lb a week. Do not go below 1,200 calories (women) or 1,500 (men) without medical supervision. Find your TDEE with the calorie calculator.

How a calorie deficit works

Your body burns energy all day. That total is your TDEE (total daily energy expenditure): resting energy (BMR) plus everything you do. If you eat less than your TDEE, your body makes up the difference from stored energy, mainly body fat. That gap is the deficit.

One pound of body fat holds roughly 3,500 calories (about 7,700 per kilogram). So a 500 calorie deficit every day adds up to 3,500 a week, or about one pound.

Health bodies commonly describe about 1 to 2 pounds a week as a steady, sustainable pace. Smaller people have less room to cut, so a 1,000 calorie deficit that works for a heavier person can push a smaller person below a safe intake. Another way to size it is a percentage: many people use 10 to 25 percent of TDEE. For 2,200 calories, that is 220 to 550.

Two worked examples

Both use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for BMR, then multiply by an activity factor to get TDEE.

Example 1: a 30-year-old woman, 165 cm, 65 kg, lightly active

  • BMR = 10 × 65 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 30 − 161 = 1,370 (rounded)
  • TDEE = 1,370 × 1.375 = 1,884
  • 250 calorie deficit: 1,634 calories a day, about 0.5 lb a week
  • 500 calorie deficit: 1,384 calories a day, about 1 lb a week, still above 1,200

Example 2: a 40-year-old man, 178 cm, 100 kg, lightly active

  • BMR = 10 × 100 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 40 + 5 = 1,918 (rounded)
  • TDEE = 1,918 × 1.375 = 2,637
  • 500 calorie deficit: 2,137 calories a day, about 1 lb a week
  • To lose 20 lb at that pace: about 20 weeks, though the number will drift as weight falls

Choose the activity factor honestly. The usual multipliers are 1.2 (little exercise), 1.375 (light), 1.55 (moderate), 1.725 (hard) and 1.9 (very hard). Overestimating it is the most common reason a plan seems to stop working. Different BMR formulas also give slightly different results, which the BMR formulas guide explains.

Why the 3,500 rule is only an estimate

The 3,500 calories per pound figure is a handy approximation, not an exact law. In real life:

  • Your TDEE falls as you get lighter. A smaller body burns less, so the same intake gives a smaller deficit over time. Recalculate after every 10 pounds (about 4.5 kg) lost.
  • Your body adapts. Energy use can drop a little more than weight alone predicts, and everyday movement often decreases without you noticing.
  • Water shifts the scale. Salt, carbohydrate intake and hormones can move weight by a pound or more from day to day.
  • Food labels and logging are imprecise. Even careful tracking is often off by 10 percent or more.

Treat the first estimate as a starting point. Track your weight for 2 to 4 weeks, then adjust by 100 to 200 calories if the trend is off.

The lowest sensible intake

Many guidelines treat 1,200 calories for women and 1,500 for men as the lowest intake to use without a doctor or dietitian. Going lower makes it harder to meet nutrient needs and raises the odds of fatigue, muscle loss and rebound eating. If your calculated target falls below these numbers, choose a smaller deficit or a longer timeline instead, as in Example 1 above.

Who should not diet without medical advice

Anyone who is pregnant or breastfeeding, under 18, has a history of disordered eating, or has a medical condition such as diabetes or heart disease should talk to a doctor or registered dietitian first. If food or weight feels stressful or out of control, ask for professional support. This guide gives estimates, not a treatment plan.

Cut food, move more, or both

You can make a deficit from eating less, moving more, or a mix. A mix is usually easier to sustain. For example, a 500 calorie deficit could be 300 from walking and 200 from small food changes.

Protecting muscle while you lose fat

When you eat less, you can lose some muscle along with fat. Two habits help limit that: keep your protein intake adequate and keep doing some resistance exercise. Many dietitians suggest more protein during weight loss than the basic minimum, often in the range of about 1.2 to 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight, but your own needs depend on your health, so check with a professional if you are unsure. When weight loss stalls

  1. Wait two to three weeks. Scale weight is noisy, so judge by the weekly average, not a single day.
  2. Recheck your numbers. Update your weight in the calculator, because your TDEE is now lower.
  3. Audit your logging. Weekends, oils, drinks and bites often add several hundred calories.
  4. Add movement before cutting more food. An extra 1,000 to 2,000 steps a day is a gentler lever than a deeper cut.
  5. Plan a maintenance break. A week or two at maintenance can make a long diet easier to finish.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I cut to lose weight?
Start with 250 to 500 calories below your TDEE. That usually gives about 0.5 to 1 pound (0.25 to 0.5 kg) of loss a week. Avoid going below 1,200 calories (women) or 1,500 (men) without medical supervision.
How big a calorie deficit is safe?
For most adults, a deficit of 500 to 1,000 calories a day, or about 1 to 2 pounds a week, is considered steady and sustainable, as long as intake stays above the lowest sensible level. Smaller people should use a smaller deficit.
How many calories do I need to eat to lose 1 pound a week?
Eat about 500 calories below your TDEE. For a TDEE of 2,200 that is about 1,700 calories a day.
Is 1,200 calories a day enough to lose weight?
It will cause weight loss for most people, but it is a floor, not a target. It can be hard to get enough nutrients and protein, so use it only as a lower limit and talk to a professional if you plan to go that low.
How long will it take to lose 20 pounds?
At a steady 500 calorie daily deficit, about 20 weeks. Real timelines vary, because your TDEE falls as you lose weight and water weight shifts the scale.
Why is the 3,500 calorie rule not exact?
It assumes a fixed energy content for lost weight and a fixed TDEE. In practice your TDEE drops as you get lighter and your body adapts, so weight loss slows over time. Use it as a starting estimate and adjust from your results.
Should I eat less or exercise more?
A mix works best for most people. Exercise raises your TDEE, builds muscle and helps you keep the weight off, while diet changes are usually the faster lever. Choose the combination you can keep up.

Conclusion

Start with your TDEE, subtract 250 to 500 calories, stay above the 1,200 and 1,500 floors, and judge progress over 2 to 4 weeks before changing anything. Update your numbers as you lose weight, and use the calorie calculator to set a target that matches your goal and timeline.

This guide gives general estimates and is not medical or dietary advice. Individual calorie needs vary. Consult a doctor or registered dietitian before starting a weight-loss plan, especially if you have a medical condition.
