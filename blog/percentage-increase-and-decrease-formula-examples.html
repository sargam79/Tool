Percentage Increase and Decrease: The Formula, Worked Examples and the Traps

📅 November 2, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ ToolPond

How to calculate percentage change, undo it, and why going up 50% then down 50% does not get you back

Percentage change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100. A positive result is a percentage increase and a negative result is a percentage decrease. Always divide by the old (starting) value, which is the step people most often get wrong.

Quick answer

Going from 80 to 100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 = +25%. Going from 100 back to 80 is (80 − 100) ÷ 100 = −20%. The same 20-unit gap gives two different percentages because the starting value is different. Check your own numbers with the percentage calculator.

The formula

Percentage change, also called relative change or percent change, compares a new value with an old one as a share of the old one:

Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100

  • If the result is positive, it is a percentage increase.
  • If the result is negative, it is a percentage decrease. Many people drop the minus sign and say "a 20% decrease".
  • The old value is the base. If the old value is zero, the percentage change cannot be calculated.

This guide goes deep on percentage change. For the full set of percentage formulas, such as percent of a number, discounts and tips, see how to calculate percentages.

The multiplier shortcut

To apply a percentage change directly, turn it into a multiplier:

  • Increase by r%: new value = old × (1 + r ÷ 100). A 20% increase is × 1.20.
  • Decrease by r%: new value = old × (1 − r ÷ 100). A 20% decrease is × 0.80.

Example: $250 increased by 12% is 250 × 1.12 = $280. $250 decreased by 12% is 250 × 0.88 = $220. Repeated changes multiply, so three years of 5% growth is 1.05 × 1.05 × 1.05 = 1.157625, an overall rise of about 15.76%, not 15%. This is the same idea as compound growth in how compound interest works.

Reverse percentages: find the original

If you know the final value after a percentage change, divide by the multiplier to get the original:

  • A price is $180 after a 20% increase. Original = 180 ÷ 1.20 = $150 (not 180 − 20% of 180 = $144).
  • A price is $80 after a 20% discount. Original = 80 ÷ 0.80 = $100 (not 80 + 20% of 80 = $96).

The percentage was taken from the original, so you must divide by the multiplier rather than apply the percentage to the new number.

Why up 50% then down 50% is not zero

Percentages are always measured against the current base. Start with 100:

  1. Increase by 50%: 100 × 1.5 = 150.
  2. Decrease by 50%: 150 × 0.5 = 75.

You end up 25% below where you started. A loss needs a bigger percentage gain to recover, because the gain is applied to a smaller base. The recovery needed after a loss L is L ÷ (1 − L):

LossLeft from 100Gain needed to get back

20%8025.00%

33.3%66.750.00%

75%25300.00%

This is why a 50% drop in an investment needs a 100% gain to recover, and why large drops are so hard to undo.

Percentage points vs percent

A percentage point is the plain difference between two percentages. Percent change is the relative difference. If an interest rate moves from 4% to 6%:

  • It rose by 2 percentage points (6 − 4).
  • It rose by 50% in relative terms ((6 − 4) ÷ 4).

Headlines often mix the two, so check which one a figure means before you compare.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by the new value instead of the old one.
  • Assuming equal up and down percentages cancel. They do not.
  • Adding successive percentages. Two 10% increases make 21%, not 20%, because 1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21.
  • Reversing a percentage from the wrong number. Divide by the multiplier, do not subtract the percentage of the new price.
  • Mixing percent and percentage points.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate percentage increase?
Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, then multiply by 100. For example, from 80 to 100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.
How do you calculate percentage decrease?
Use the same formula. A negative result means a decrease. From 60 to 45 is (45 − 60) ÷ 60 × 100 = −25%, a 25% decrease.
What is the formula for percentage change?
Percentage change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100. The old value is the base you divide by.
Why is a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease not back to the start?
Each percentage applies to a different base. 100 up 50% is 150, and 150 down 50% is 75, which is 25% below the start.
How do you work out the original price after a percentage increase?
Divide the new price by the multiplier. If the price is $180 after a 20% increase, the original is 180 ÷ 1.20 = $150.
What is the difference between percent and percentage points?
Percentage points are the simple difference between two percentages, while percent change is the relative difference. Going from 4% to 6% is 2 percentage points, or a 50% increase.
How do you add a percentage to a number?
Multiply the number by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. Adding 12% to 250 is 250 × 1.12 = 280.
Can percentage change be more than 100%?
Yes. An increase can be any size, so going from 50 to 150 is +200%. A decrease cannot exceed 100% for positive values, because the new value would be below zero.

Conclusion

Percentage change is the difference divided by the old value, times 100. Use multipliers to apply it, divide by the multiplier to undo it, and remember that up and down percentages do not cancel.
