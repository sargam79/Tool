Western vs Korean Age Explained: Why One Person Can Have Two Ages

📅 Updated November 15, 2026⏱️ 7 min read✍️ ToolPond

International age counts birthdays; traditional Korean age starts at 1 and adds a year each 1 January. Here is how to convert and what changed in 2023.

In Korea, you can be one or two years older than you are on your passport. The reason is that two different counting traditions have lived side by side: the international system most of the world uses, and a traditional count in which you are already 1 at birth and gain a year every New Year. This guide explains how each works, how to convert between them, and what changed in 2023. To check your own numbers, the age calculator shows international and traditional Korean age together.

⚡ Quick answer

Korean age = current year − birth year + 1. International age is the number of birthdays you have had. So Korean age is international age + 1 if your birthday has already passed this year, and + 2 if it has not. Example: born 15 May 1990, on 8 October 2026 you are 36 internationally and 37 in the traditional Korean count.

International (Western) age

International age, also called completed years, starts at 0 on the day you are born and increases by 1 on each birthday. It is what most countries, airlines, hospitals and legal systems use. In Korean it is called man nai (만 나이), where man means "full" or "completed". The age calculator uses this system for its main result.

Traditional Korean age

The traditional count, seun nai (세는 나이), means "counting age". A baby is 1 at birth, and everyone gains a year on 1 January, regardless of their birthday. It is often explained as counting the time before birth, as treating the year of birth as the first year, and as a shared New Year when the whole community grows older together.

The effect is that a baby born on 31 December is 1 year old at birth and 2 the very next day, while the international age is still 0. Over a whole year, the traditional age is either 1 or 2 years above the international age.

Year age is simply the current year minus the birth year, so everyone born in the same calendar year shares one age. That makes group rules simple, such as a single cut-off for school entry or for buying alcohol, which is why it survived alongside the other two.

What changed in June 2023

In June 2023 South Korea moved most legal and administrative uses of age to the international system, so that laws, contracts and official documents use one consistent age. The change reduced confusion in areas such as medical care, insurance and public services. It did not change social habits: many people still say and ask for the traditional age in everyday conversation, and some rules still count by year age. If a Korean form or service matters for your situation, check which age it uses.

Elsewhere in East Asia

Similar customs exist across the region. The Chinese xusui (虚岁) count also starts at 1, but traditionally adds a year at Lunar New Year rather than 1 January, so it depends on a date that moves every year. A similar practice, tuoi mu, is known in Vietnam. Japan once had a counting-age tradition too, but a 1950 law encouraged the international method and it has largely disappeared. Because Lunar New Year shifts from year to year, the age calculator shows only the calendar-year Korean count.

Which age should you use?

  • Passports, visas, contracts, medical forms: international age (completed years).
  • Talking with Korean friends or family: either can be meant, so ask. "Man nai" means international age.
  • Eligibility rules: use the exact wording and cut-off date the organisation publishes.
  • Comparing ages: use total days or the age difference calculator.

Conclusion

Frequently asked questions

What is Korean age?
Korean age, or seun nai, is the traditional count in which a baby is 1 at birth and everyone adds a year on 1 January. It is usually one or two years higher than international age.
How do I convert Korean age to international age?
Subtract 1 if your birthday has already happened this year, or subtract 2 if it has not. Example: a person born in May is 1 year younger in international age after May, and 2 years younger before May.
Did South Korea abolish Korean age?
South Korea moved official and legal ages to the international system in June 2023. The traditional count is no longer used in most laws and government paperwork, but many people still use it in conversation.
What is yeon nai, the Korean year age?
Year age is the current year minus the birth year, so everyone born in the same calendar year has the same age. Some youth-protection and military rules, such as those for alcohol, tobacco and service, still use this style of count.
Do China, Japan and Vietnam use the same system?
Similar traditions exist. The Chinese xusui count and the Vietnamese tuoi mu count add a year around Lunar New Year. Japan used a counting-age tradition, but a 1950 law encouraged the international method and it has largely disappeared.
Which age should I use on forms?
Use international age (completed years) unless a form clearly asks for something else. When the form is Korean and asks for "만 나이" (man nai), that is the international age.
