How to Calculate Percentages: Formulas, Examples & Common Mistakes

📅 October 6, 2026⏱️ 9 min read✍️ ToolPond Pro

Every percentage question is one of a few patterns. Learn the formula for each, with worked examples and practice problems.

Percentages show up everywhere: discounts, tips, taxes, grades, interest rates, pay rises. The good news is that almost every percentage question is one of a handful of patterns, and each pattern has a one-line formula. This guide walks through each one with worked examples, then covers the mistakes that trip people up most. You can check any answer with our free percentage calculator.

What a percentage is

A percentage is a number written as a part of 100. The word comes from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred". So 25% means 25 out of every 100, which is the same as the fraction 25/100, the simplified fraction 1/4, and the decimal 0.25.

To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by 100 (move the decimal point two places left). To turn a decimal back into a percentage, multiply by 100. Every formula below is built on this one idea.

How to find a percentage of a number

📐 Formula
Result = Number × (Percentage ÷ 100)

Example: what is 15% of 240?

  1. Convert 15% to a decimal: 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15.
  2. Multiply: 240 × 0.15 = 36.

The same method works for a tip, a commission or a tax amount. A 20% tip on a bill of 85 is 85 × 0.20 = 17.

How to find what percent one number is of another

📐 Formula
Percent = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Example: you scored 45 out of 180 on a test. What percent is that?

  1. Divide the part by the whole: 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25.
  2. Multiply by 100: 0.25 × 100 = 25%.

Always put the part on top and the whole on the bottom. If you reverse them, you get 400% instead of 25%, which is a sign something went wrong. Try it with the what percent is X of Y calculator.

Percentage increase and decrease

📐 Formula
Percentage change = ((New − Original) ÷ Original) × 100

A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

Increase example: a price rises from 80 to 100.

(100 − 80) ÷ 80 = 0.25, so the price rose 25%.

Decrease example: the same price falls from 100 back to 80.

(80 − 100) ÷ 100 = −0.20, so the price fell 20%.

💡 Why the two answers differ

The gap is 20 in both cases, but the starting point is different. The percentage is always measured against the original value, so the same gap is a bigger percentage of a smaller starting number. Use the percentage change calculator to try your own numbers.

Percentage difference vs percentage change

Percentage change has a clear starting point. Percentage difference is for comparing two numbers when neither one is "the original", such as two prices from two shops or two measurements.

📐 Formula
Difference = |A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100

Example: compare 50 and 70.

  1. The gap is |50 − 70| = 20.
  2. The average is (50 + 70) ÷ 2 = 60.
  3. 20 ÷ 60 × 100 = 33.3333%.

Treating 50 as the starting point instead would give a percentage change of 40%. Both are valid, but they answer different questions. The percentage difference calculator gives the same answer whichever number you enter first.

How to add or subtract a percentage

📐 Formulas
Add: Number × (1 + Percentage ÷ 100)
Subtract: Number × (1 − Percentage ÷ 100)

Add example (sales tax): an item costs 85 and tax is 8%. 85 × 1.08 = 91.8.

Subtract example (a markdown): a jacket costs 250 and is 15% off. 250 × 0.85 = 212.5.

This is faster than working out the percentage amount and then adding or subtracting it. Use the add or subtract a percentage calculator for quick checks.

Reverse percentages: find the original

Sometimes you know the result and the percentage, and you need the starting value.

Find the whole from a part

📐 Formula
Whole = Part ÷ (Percentage ÷ 100)

Example: 45 is 20% of what number? 45 ÷ 0.20 = 225.

Find the original price before a discount

📐 Formula
Original = Sale price ÷ (1 − Discount ÷ 100)

Example: a coat costs 68 after 15% off. 68 ÷ 0.85 = 80.

⚠️ Do not add the percentage back

Adding 15% to 68 gives 78.2, which is wrong. The 15% was taken from the original price of 80, not from 68. Use the original price calculator to avoid this.

Discounts and stacked percentages

A discount calculation is just "subtract a percentage". 25% off a price of 100 is 100 × 0.75 = 75, so you save 25.

Stacked discounts do not add up. Take 20% off, then another 10% off:

  1. After 20% off: 100 × 0.80 = 80.
  2. After another 10% off: 80 × 0.90 = 72.

The total saving is 28%, not 30%, because the second discount is applied to the lower price. The same rule applies to a percentage of a percentage: 20% of 50% is (20 × 50) ÷ 100 = 10%.

Common percentage mistakes

1. Using the wrong base

Increase 100 by 50% and you get 150. Now decrease that by 50% and you get 75, not 100. To get back to 100 from 150 you need a decrease of 33.3333%. Percentages are only reversible when you remember each one is measured from a different starting number.

2. Confusing percent with percentage points

If an interest rate goes from 4% to 6%, it rose by 2 percentage points, which is a 50% increase in the rate. News and financial reports mix these up often, so check which one is meant.

3. Adding stacked discounts

As shown above, 20% and then 10% off is 28% off in total, not 30%.

4. Dividing by the wrong number

For "what percent is X of Y", the whole goes on the bottom. For percentage change, the original goes on the bottom. Writing down which number is the base before you calculate prevents most errors.

Practice problems

Try each one first, then open the answer.

1. What is 35% of 260?

91. 260 × 0.35 = 91.

2. 18 is what percent of 24?

75%. 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75, and 0.75 × 100 = 75%.

3. A salary goes from 40,000 to 43,000. What is the percentage increase?

7.5%. (43,000 − 40,000) ÷ 40,000 = 0.075.

4. A 120 item is on sale at 30% off, and then you add 5% tax to the sale price. What do you pay?

88.2. 120 × 0.70 = 84, and 84 × 1.05 = 88.2.

5. After a 20% discount, a bag costs 56. What was the original price?

70. 56 ÷ 0.80 = 70.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate a percentage of a number?
Multiply the number by the percentage divided by 100. For example, 15% of 240 is 240 × 0.15 = 36.
How do you find what percent one number is of another?
Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 45 out of 180 is 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%.
What is the formula for percentage increase?
Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original value, and multiply by 100. If a price goes from 80 to 100, the increase is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.
How do you work out a discount?
Multiply the price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal. A 25% discount on 100 is 100 × 0.75 = 75, so you save 25.
How do you find the original price after a discount?
Divide the sale price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal. If a coat costs 68 after 15% off, the original price is 68 ÷ 0.85 = 80.
Why is 20% off followed by 10% off not 30% off?
The second discount is taken from the already reduced price. 100 becomes 80 after 20% off, then 72 after another 10% off, so the total saving is 28%.
What is the difference between percent and percentage points?
A percentage point is the plain gap between two percentages. A rate that moves from 4% to 6% rose by 2 percentage points, which is a 50% increase in the rate.

Conclusion

Percentages become easy once you spot which pattern you are looking at: a percentage of a number, what percent one number is of another, a change from an original value, or a reverse problem that starts from the result. Write down the base before you calculate, and remember that percentages taken from different bases do not simply add or cancel. When you want a quick check, use the free percentage calculator.
