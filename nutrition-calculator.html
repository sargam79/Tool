    How this nutrition calculator works

    Pick a food from the list, enter how much you ate in grams or in normal servings, and add it to a meal. The calculator scales the food's per-100 g values to your amount and adds everything into running totals for the day. You also get fiber and net carbs (total carbs minus fiber), plus progress bars against your own calorie and macro goals.

    Worked examples

    • 150 g of cooked chicken breast = 248 kcal, 46.5 g protein, 0 g carbs, 5.4 g fat.
    • 1 cup (158 g) of cooked white rice = 205 kcal, 44.6 g carbs, 4.3 g protein, 0.5 g fat.
    • 1 cup (172 g) of cooked black beans has 40.8 g carbs and 15 g fiber, so about 25.8 g net carbs.

    How to use it

    • Search or tap a popular food and check the per-100 g values shown.
    • Choose the amount. Use grams if you weigh your food, or servings for a cup, slice or piece.
    • Add it to a meal, then repeat for the rest of the day.
    • Set your goal and watch the bars. Orange means you went over that target.
    • Missing a food? Add it with the numbers from its label.

    Understanding macronutrients

    Protein (4 calories per gram)

    Supports muscle repair, enzymes and immune function. The US RDA is 0.8 g per kg of body weight (about 56 g for a 70 kg adult), and active people often aim for 1.2–2.0 g per kg. Good sources: chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, lentils, tofu.

    Carbohydrates (4 calories per gram)

    Your body's main fuel. Whole sources such as oats, quinoa, beans and sweet potato bring fiber and steady energy. Dietary guidelines suggest roughly 45–65% of calories from carbohydrate for most adults.

    Fat (9 calories per gram)

    More than twice as calorie-dense as protein or carbs, and needed for hormones and absorbing vitamins A, D, E and K. Favor unsaturated fats like olive oil, nuts, avocado and fish. Dietary guidelines suggest roughly 20–35% of calories from fat.

    Macro presets in this calculator

    • Balanced: 25% protein, 50% carbs, 25% fat
    • Weight loss: 40% protein, 30% carbs, 30% fat. More protein helps you feel full.
    • Muscle gain: 30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat. Carbs fuel training.
    • Keto: 20% protein, 5% carbs, 75% fat. Very low carb.

    These are common starting points, not prescriptions. For a personal plan, use the macros calculator or read the macros breakdown guide.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How does this nutrition calculator work?

    Search a food, choose an amount in grams or servings, and add it to a meal. The calculator scales the per-100 g values for that food to your amount and keeps running totals for calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber and net carbs. If a food is missing, enter its label values in the custom food box.

    Where do the food values come from?

    The built-in foods use typical values from the USDA FoodData Central database, rounded to one decimal. Real foods vary by brand, ripeness, cooking method and cut, so treat the numbers as good estimates and use the package label when you have one.

    How many calories are in a gram of protein, carbs and fat?

    Protein and carbohydrate each provide about 4 calories per gram and fat provides about 9. Alcohol provides about 7. The calculator uses the calorie figure listed for each food and shows your protein, carb and fat split using the 4/4/9 values.

    What are net carbs and how are they calculated here?

    Net carbs are total carbohydrates minus dietary fiber. This calculator shows both numbers. Sugar alcohols are not tracked, so if you eat products that contain them, subtract them yourself using the label.

    Does the calculator save my food log?

    Your log and calorie goal are saved only in this browser on this device, using local storage, so they are still there when you come back. Nothing is sent to a server. Use Clear all to erase the log, or clear your browser data.

    Why do my totals differ from a food label or another app?

    Common reasons are raw versus cooked weight, different brands, rounding on labels, and different database entries for the same food. Weigh food the same way each time, either raw or cooked, and pick the matching entry.

    How much protein do I need per day?

    The US recommended dietary allowance is 0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight per day, which is about 56 g for a 70 kg adult. Active people and athletes often aim for 1.2 to 2.0 g per kg. Your needs depend on your health and goals, so ask a doctor or dietitian if you are unsure.

    Which macro split should I choose?

    The presets are common starting points, not medical advice: balanced is 25% protein, 50% carbs and 25% fat; weight loss is 40/30/30; muscle gain is 30/40/30; keto is 20/5/75. Pick one that you can follow consistently and adjust it based on how you feel and your results.
