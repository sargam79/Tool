How Creators Earn on TikTok and Instagram

Creators on TikTok and Instagram usually earn from several sources at once: platform payment programs (where available), brand sponsorships, gifts and subscriptions, and affiliate links or their own products. This calculator gives a rough monthly estimate so you can see how views, followers and engagement might translate into money. It uses simple assumptions that are shown below, not live data from either platform.

Platform programs change. TikTok's original Creator Fund was closed in several countries and replaced by newer programs with different rules, and Instagram's Reels bonus programs were limited and have ended for many accounts. Eligibility, rates and availability depend on your country and account, so check the creator tools inside each app for the current rules.

What moves the number:

  • Where your viewers are: advertisers generally pay more for viewers in some countries than others.
  • Engagement: comments, shares and watch time tell the platform and brands that people care about your content.
  • Video type and length: some programs only count longer videos or original content.
  • Sponsorships: for most creators, brand deals are worth far more than per-view payouts, but they are negotiated one by one.

Use it for planning. Try different view and follower counts to see how an extra 100,000 views, or a higher engagement rate, changes the estimate. Real payouts can be much lower or higher.

📐 Assumptions Used

  • • Region: ×1.5 US/UK/CA, ×1.2 Europe, ×0.8 global, ×0.5 Asia/Pacific
  • • Engagement over 3%: ×1.1; over 5%: ×1.2
  • • Sponsorship per post: followers × $0.02 × engagement bonus
  • • Gifts: $100 per 100,000 followers, only when you have more than 100,000 followers

🆚 TikTok vs Instagram

TikTok: strong for discovery, because videos can reach people who do not follow you.

Instagram: strong for following up with an existing audience and for brand partnerships. Many creators use both.

💡 Practical Tips for Creators

  • Do not rely on a single income source. Platform payouts can change or end.
  • Brands look at engagement and audience fit, not only follower count. Keep screenshots of your analytics.
  • Know your audience's countries; it affects both payouts and sponsorship interest.
  • Agree the deliverables, usage rights and payment date in writing before you start a paid post.
  • Follow advertising rules: label paid partnerships using the platform's disclosure tool.
  • Post consistently and review which videos keep viewers watching to the end.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does TikTok pay per 1,000 views? ▼

There is no fixed rate. TikTok's payment programs have changed over the years and differ by country, and payouts depend on the views that qualify, where viewers are and the type of video. Check the rules for your account inside the app. This calculator uses an assumed rate of $0.03 per 1,000 views for TikTok and $0.025 for Instagram, adjusted for region and engagement, so treat the result as a rough planning number.

Is the TikTok Creator Fund still available? ▼

The original Creator Fund was closed in several countries, including the US, and replaced by newer programs such as the Creativity Program and Creator Rewards Program. Availability, eligibility rules and pay differ by country and change often, so check the Creator tools in your own TikTok app for what applies to you.

Does Instagram pay for Reels views? ▼

Instagram has run limited bonus programs for Reels in the past, but they were invite-only and many ended. Most Instagram creators earn from brand deals, subscriptions, gifts and affiliate links rather than from a per-view payout. Check what your account is offered in Instagram's professional dashboard.

How does this calculator estimate my earnings? ▼

Payout = monthly views ÷ 1,000 × base rate × region multiplier × engagement bonus. Base rate is $0.03 for TikTok and $0.025 for Instagram. Region multipliers are 1.5 (US/UK/Canada), 1.2 (Europe), 0.5 (Asia/Pacific) and 0.8 (global mix). Engagement above 3% adds 10% and above 5% adds 20%. Sponsorship per post = followers × $0.02 × the same engagement bonus. Gifts are estimated only above 100,000 followers. All of these are assumptions, not platform data.

What is a good engagement rate? ▼

This tool uses (likes + comments + shares) ÷ views × 100. Rates differ widely by platform, niche and audience size, and smaller accounts often have higher rates. Compare yourself against your own past videos rather than a single benchmark.

How much can a creator charge for a sponsored post? ▼

Rates are negotiated and vary a lot by niche, audience location, engagement and the deliverables. The calculator's figure uses a simple rule of $0.02 per follower, adjusted for engagement. Use it as a starting point for a conversation, not as a rate card, and check what comparable creators in your niche charge.

Does the calculator send my numbers anywhere? ▼

No. The calculation runs in your browser and your numbers are not sent to our server. The site may show ads and uses analytics, as described in our Privacy Policy.
