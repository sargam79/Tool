Understanding Your Mortgage: The Math Behind Your Home Loan

Example: $240,000 loan at today's live 30-year average rate

A mortgage is likely the largest financial commitment of your life. Furthermore, understanding how your loan works — what you pay in principal versus interest — is essential for making informed decisions about homeownership.

What is a mortgage? A mortgage is a loan to purchase real estate, secured by the property itself. In addition, you borrow money upfront and pay it back over time with interest. Unlike other loans, mortgages typically have very long terms of 15 to 30 years.

How much of my payment is interest vs principal? In the early years of a mortgage, most of your payment goes to interest. However, as time goes on, increasingly more goes to principal. At today's live rate shown above, your first payment on a typical loan is often 75-80% interest and only 20-25% principal. As a result, understanding the amortization schedule is crucial.

What affects mortgage payments? Four main factors determine your monthly payment. First, the loan amount — a larger loan means a larger payment. Additionally, the interest rate has a significant impact, and rates currently sit well above the 2021-2022 lows. Moreover, the loan term matters greatly. Finally, your down payment determines the loan size.

The Mortgage Payment Formula

Monthly Payment Formula (P&I):

M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]

Where:

M = Monthly payment

P = Principal (loan amount)

r = Monthly interest rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100)

n = Total number of payments (years × 12)

This is the standard amortization formula used by all lenders. Furthermore, your total monthly payment also includes property tax, insurance, and HOA fees if applicable. As a result, the total cost is often higher than most buyers initially expect.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much house can I afford? ▼

A common rule is that your monthly housing payment should not exceed 28% of gross income. If you earn $6,000 per month, your housing payment should be under $1,680. Use this calculator to test what income level works for your target price and rate.

What is PMI and when can I remove it? ▼

PMI is required if your down payment is less than 20%. You can remove PMI once you have paid down the loan to 80% of the home's value, which usually takes 8 to 12 years at today's typical rates.

Should I get a 15-year or 30-year mortgage? ▼

A 15-year mortgage has a higher monthly payment, typically 25-30% more, but you pay roughly half the total interest. A 30-year mortgage offers lower monthly payments but costs significantly more in interest over the life of the loan. Use this calculator to compare both at today's actual rates.

How much interest will I pay in total? ▼

This calculator shows total interest in the results using the rate shown on the page. At a recent average 30-year rate, a $300,000 loan typically costs around $430,000-$450,000 in total interest over the full term — which is why extra principal payments save so much money.

Should I refinance my mortgage? ▼

Refinancing generally makes sense if current rates are at least 0.75-1% lower than your existing rate, and you plan to stay in the home long enough to recoup closing costs of roughly $3,000 to $6,000, which typically takes 2 to 3 years to break even.

What is the average mortgage rate today? ▼

Mortgage rates change daily and are shown live at the top of this calculator, sourced from Freddie Mac's weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey. As of early October 2026, the 30-year fixed average is around 7.2-7.4%, and the 15-year fixed average is around 6.6-6.8%.
