Common questions about using the scientific calculator and its features.

A scientific calculator performs advanced mathematical functions beyond basic arithmetic, including trigonometry (sin, cos, tan), logarithms, exponents, factorials, permutations and combinations. Unlike a standard calculator, it supports longer expressions with parentheses, memory functions, and scientific notation. This makes it essential for students, engineers, scientists, and anyone working with advanced math.

Use the EXP or E button to enter scientific notation. For example, to enter 3.5 x 10^8, press 3.5, then EXP (or type E), then 8. The calculator displays it as 3.5E8 and computes it as 350,000,000. You can also type directly using the E key on your keyboard.

How to Use an Online Scientific Calculator

Quick answer: type your expression with brackets, check that the angle mode says DEG (degrees) or RAD (radians), then press =. In degree mode sin(30) = 0.5; in radian mode sin(30) = -0.988. Most wrong trig answers come from the wrong angle mode.

  1. Choose DEG for angles in degrees (the default) or RAD for radians. Press Ctrl+D to switch on a keyboard.
  2. Type or tap your expression. Functions open a bracket for you, for example sin(. Close every bracket yourself.
  3. Press = or Enter. The result is saved in the history list (last 20 calculations); click an entry to reuse its result.
  4. Use M+, M−, MR and MC to add to, subtract from, recall and clear the memory value.

Degrees or radians?

Degrees split a full turn into 360. Radians measure the angle by arc length, so a full turn is 2π (about 6.283). School geometry usually uses degrees; calculus, physics and programming usually use radians. To convert, multiply degrees by π/180. If sin(30) shows 0.5 you are in degree mode; if it shows -0.988 you are in radian mode.

Why do I see Error?

  • An unclosed or extra bracket, such as sin(30.
  • Dividing by zero, a square root or logarithm of a number that is out of range, or an inverse trig input outside -1 to 1.
  • Unsupported symbols. Use fact(5) rather than 5!, and mod(10,3) rather than 10 mod 3.

Limits of this calculator

It handles one expression at a time. It has no graphing, equation solver, matrix or statistics mode. Very large or very small results switch to scientific notation. Results are shown to about 12 significant digits, so tiny rounding differences can appear. For exams, check your own calculator rules.
