What is a Body Fat Calculator?

A body fat calculator is a free online tool that calculates your body fat percentage based on your physical measurements. Unlike a BMI calculator, which only considers height and weight, a body fat percentage calculator provides a more accurate assessment of your body composition by distinguishing between muscle mass and fat mass.

Our calculator uses multiple proven formulas including the Navy method and BMI-based calculations to give you a comprehensive understanding of your body composition. This information is valuable for fitness tracking, weight loss goals, and overall health monitoring.

How to Use the Body Fat Calculator

Step 1: Select Your Gender

Choose whether you are male or female. Different formulas apply to each gender due to natural biological differences in body composition.

Step 2: Enter Your Measurements

Provide your age, height, weight, and body measurements. The calculator requires:

  • Neck measurement: Measure just below your larynx (Adam's apple)
  • Waist measurement: Measure at your navel level, not where your pants sit
  • Hip measurement (women only): Measure at the widest point of your hips

Step 3: Get Instant Results

Our calculator instantly computes your body fat percentage using the Navy formula and BMI method, showing you your lean body mass and fat mass in pounds or kilograms, plus the ideal body fat for your age.

Body Fat Calculation Methods Explained

Navy Method

The Navy body fat calculation formula is widely used and validated with military personnel. For men, it uses neck and waist circumference. For women, it uses neck, waist, and hip measurements. Because it uses body circumferences rather than weight alone, it usually tracks body fat better than the BMI method, but it is still an estimate.

BMI-Based Method

This method uses the BMI formula combined with age and gender to estimate body fat percentage. While less precise than the Navy method, it provides a quick estimation using only height and weight.

Body Fat Formulas

The calculator uses the U.S. Navy circumference method developed by Hodgdon and Beckett. Measurements are in inches for the US version and in centimeters for the metric version.

Navy method (US units, inches)

Men: BFP = 86.010 × log10(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log10(height) + 36.76
Women: BFP = 163.205 × log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log10(height) − 78.387

Navy method (metric, centimeters)

Men: BFP = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450
Women: BFP = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450

BMI method

Adult men: BFP = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 16.2
Adult women: BFP = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 5.4
Boys under 18: BFP = 1.51 × BMI − 0.70 × age − 2.2  |  Girls under 18: BFP = 1.51 × BMI − 0.70 × age + 1.4

Fat mass and lean mass

Fat mass = body fat % × weight  |  Lean mass = weight − fat mass

Worked example (man, US units): height 70 in, neck 15 in, waist 32 in, weight 180 lbs. Waist − neck = 17, so BFP = 86.010 × 1.2304 − 70.041 × 1.8451 + 36.76 = 13.4%. Fat mass = 180 × 13.4% = 24.0 lbs, and lean mass = 180 − 24.0 = 156.0 lbs.

Fat to lose to reach ideal: assuming your lean mass stays the same, your target weight is lean mass ÷ (1 − ideal body fat), and the fat to lose is your current weight minus that target.

Limits of Body Fat Calculators and Medical Disclaimer

Tape-measure formulas estimate body fat and cannot match clinical methods such as DEXA scans or hydrostatic weighing. Results can be less reliable for very muscular people, very lean people, older adults and during pregnancy. Body fat percentage also works best alongside other measures such as BMI.

This calculator gives general information only and is not medical advice. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before making health decisions. Categories follow the American Council on Exercise (ACE), and the Navy method is described in the U.S. Navy physical readiness program.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ How is body fat percentage calculated?

This calculator uses two estimates. The U.S. Navy method uses your height plus neck and waist circumference (and hip circumference for women) in a logarithmic formula. The BMI method uses your BMI, age and sex. Fat mass is body fat percentage multiplied by body weight, and lean mass is the rest.

❓ What is a healthy body fat percentage?

By the American Council on Exercise (ACE) categories, men are in the fitness range at 14-17% and average at 18-24%, while women are in the fitness range at 21-24% and average at 25-31%. Essential fat is 2-5% for men and 10-13% for women, and levels at or below that should be discussed with a doctor. Ideal levels also rise with age.

❓ Which body fat calculation method is most accurate?

DEXA scans and hydrostatic weighing are more accurate than any tape-measure formula, but they are costly and impractical for regular use. The Navy method is a practical estimate that can still be off by several percentage points for an individual. The BMI method is rougher because it only uses height, weight and age, so treat both as estimates and compare them over time.

❓ Can I use this body fat calculator for fitness tracking?

Yes. Measuring the same way every month shows whether your waist and body composition are changing, which the scale alone cannot tell you. Look at the trend across several readings rather than any single number.

❓ How do I take accurate body measurements?

Use a soft tape measure, ideally in the morning before eating, and keep it snug but not tight. Measure the neck just below the larynx, the waist at the navel for men (or the narrowest point for women), and the hips at their widest point. Do not pull your stomach in, and measure to the nearest quarter inch (0.5 cm).

❓ What is the difference between body fat percentage and BMI?

BMI only uses height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat. Two people with the same BMI can have very different body fat percentages. Body fat percentage estimates how much of your weight is fat, which makes it more informative for fitness, although BMI remains a useful quick screening measure.

❓ Is this body fat calculator accurate for everyone?

No estimate is perfect. Tape-measure formulas can be less reliable for very muscular people, for people with very low body fat, for older adults, and during pregnancy. If you need a precise figure, ask a healthcare professional about a DEXA scan or another clinical method.

❓ How often should I measure my body fat percentage?

Once a month is enough for most people. Weekly readings can swing because of water retention and small measuring differences. Measure at the same time of day and in the same way each time so the changes you see are real.

❓ What is ideal body fat for my age?

The calculator shows an ideal body fat percentage for your age and sex using the Jackson and Pollock reference values, which rise gradually with age. For example, the reference for a 30-year-old is 12.7% for men and 19.3% for women. Ages under 20 use the age-20 value and ages over 55 use the age-55 value.

❓ Can I use metric units?

Yes. Switch between US units (inches and pounds) and metric units (centimeters and kilograms) with the buttons above the form. Your values are converted for you, and results show in the unit you choose.
