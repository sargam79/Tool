Calculate your age

Exact age

Quick answerYour age is the time between your date of birth and a chosen date (usually today), counted in completed years, then months, then days. Someone born on 15 May 1990 is 36 years, 4 months and 23 days old on 8 October 2026, which is 13,295 days.

What is an age calculator?

An age calculator works out the time that has passed between a date of birth and another date. The standard result is chronological age: the number of completed years, plus the months and days since the last birthday. This one also converts the same span into total months, weeks, days, hours, minutes and seconds, and tells you when your next birthday falls.

Because you can change the "Age at the date of" box, it answers two different questions with one tool: how old am I today? and how old was (or will) I be on a particular date? The second question is the one people need for school cut-off dates, eligibility checks, forms, anniversaries and planning ahead.

How to calculate age from date of birth

The calculator does this in a fraction of a second, but the method is simple enough to check by hand. You count in three steps: whole years, whole months, then days.

Age = completed years + completed months + remaining days
Years = current year − birth year, minus 1 if the birthday has not yet happened this year

Worked example 1: 15 May 1990 to 8 October 2026

  1. Years: 2026 − 1990 = 36. The 15 May birthday has already passed in 2026, so the answer stays 36.
  2. Months: count forward from 15 May 2026 to 15 September 2026, which is 4 whole months.
  3. Days: from 15 September to 8 October is 15 days left in September plus 8 days in October, so 23 days.

Result: 36 years, 4 months, 23 days, or 13,295 days (1,899 weeks and 2 days).

Worked example 2: borrowing days from the previous month

Born 28 November 1995, measured on 5 March 2026. The year gap is 31, but the November birthday has not yet come round in 2026, so the completed years are 30. From 28 November 2025 to 28 February 2026 is 3 months. From 28 February to 5 March is 5 days (February 2026 has 28 days). Result: 30 years, 3 months, 5 days (11,055 days in total).

The key rule is to count months from the last day-of-month match, not by subtracting month numbers. That is why a birthday on the 31st needs a clear rule for shorter months (see common mistakes). How to calculate your age on a specific date

To find an age at a date other than today, set the second box, "Age at the date of", to the date you care about. Everything else, including the totals and the next-birthday card, is then measured from that date. This is the quickest way to answer questions such as:

  • Looking back: how old was I on the date of a past event, a graduation or an application deadline?
  • Looking ahead: how old will I be on 1 January 2035, on a planned retirement date or at a wedding?
  • Eligibility cut-offs: how old will a child be on a school's entry date, and how old must someone be on the day of a competition or exam? Cut-off dates differ by school, region and organisation, so use the date they publish.
  • Records and paperwork: what age should be written on a form that asks for age "as of" a particular date?

If the chosen date is before the date of birth, the calculator asks you to correct it instead of showing a negative age. If you are planning ahead for work, the retirement calculator turns a target age into savings and income estimates.

Age in days, weeks, months and hours

Total days is the most precise way to compare ages, because it does not depend on uneven month lengths. The calculator counts every calendar day between the two dates, then derives weeks (days ÷ 7), months (completed calendar months) and hours, minutes and seconds from the same span.

One average calendar year is 365.2425 days in the Gregorian calendar, which is why a round number of years never gives a round number of days. Use the table to see how some popular milestones line up.

Western age vs Korean and East Asian age

Most of the world uses international age, also called completed years: you are 0 at birth and add a year on each birthday. Several East Asian traditions count differently, which is why the same person can have two ages at once. The calculator shows the traditional Korean count as an extra figure under your result.

SystemAge at birthWhen a year is addedWhere it is used
International (completed years)0On each birthdayMost countries; the official system in South Korea since June 2023
Korean traditional count (seun nai)1Every 1 JanuaryEveryday and social use in Korea
Korean year age (yeon nai)0Every 1 January (current year − birth year)Some youth-protection and military rules
Chinese traditional count (xusui)1Lunar New YearSome traditional and family settings

A baby born on 31 December is 1 year old in the traditional Korean count the next day, while the international age is still 0. That is why the Korean figure is usually one or two years higher than the international one. Lunar-calendar systems depend on the date of Lunar New Year, which changes every year, so this page shows only the Korean count based on the calendar year. Leap years and 29 February birthdays

A year is a leap year when it is divisible by 4, except century years, which must be divisible by 400. So 2024 and 2000 were leap years, but 1900 was not. Leap years add 29 February, which is why the Gregorian calendar averages 365.2425 days per year (97 leap years every 400 years).

People born on 29 February are sometimes called leaplings; the odds of a leap-day birth are about 1 in 1,461. In non-leap years this calculator counts 28 February as their birthday. Legal systems differ: some countries treat 1 March as the day a leapling reaches a legal age, so check the local rule for any deadline that matters.

Common mistakes when calculating age

  • Counting both the start and end date. Age counts the days that have passed, so the difference between consecutive dates is 1 day, not 2.
  • Subtracting years only. The year difference is one too high until the birthday has happened this year.
  • Ignoring month length. A person born on 31 January is not "1 month old" on 28 February in most counting rules. This calculator clamps to the last day of a shorter month, so 31 January plus one month is 28 (or 29) February.
  • Mixing age systems. International age and traditional Korean age can differ by up to two years for the same person.
  • Using the wrong reference date. A form may ask for age "as of" a deadline, not today. Set "Age at the date of" to that date.
  • Trusting rounded day counts. Multiplying years by 365 ignores leap days. Count calendar days instead.

When do you need an exact age?

  • School and childcare: entry-date eligibility, year-group placement and age-group sports.
  • Government and legal forms: passports, licences, voting and other minimum-age rules, always checked against the official criteria.
  • Work and pensions: job age limits, retirement dates and pension eligibility.
  • Health records: paediatric growth tracking, screening schedules and age-based reference ranges, such as the age input in a calorie calculator or BMI calculator.
  • Celebrations and curiosity: milestone birthdays, your 10,000th day, or how many days you have been alive.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my age from my date of birth?

Subtract your birth year from the current year, then subtract one more year if your birthday has not happened yet this year. For the months and days, count forward from your last birthday: whole months first, then the leftover days. Or enter your date of birth in the calculator above to get years, months and days at once.

How do I calculate my age on a specific date?

Enter your date of birth, then type the date you want in the "Age at the date of" box. The calculator measures from your birth date to that date, in the past or the future, so it answers questions like how old you were on 1 January 2010 or how old you will be on 1 January 2035.

How many days old am I?

Enter your date of birth and read the "Total days" figure. It counts every calendar day from your birth date to the chosen date, leap days included. As a rough guide, a 30-year-old is about 10,957 days old (30 × 365.2425).

How many days are left until my next birthday?

After you enter your date of birth, the "Next birthday" card shows the date, the weekday and the number of days remaining, plus the age you will turn. On your birthday the countdown shows 0 days.

How is a leap day (29 February) birthday handled?

In years that are not leap years, this calculator treats 28 February as the birthday and adds the extra year on that date. Legal treatment differs by country: some places treat 1 March as the day a person born on 29 February comes of age, so check the local rule for any legal deadline.

What is Korean age and how is it different?

In the traditional Korean count a baby is 1 at birth and gains a year every 1 January, so Korean age is usually one or two years higher than international age. South Korea moved official and legal ages to the international system in June 2023, but the traditional count is still used in everyday conversation. The calculator shows it as an extra result.

Can I use this age calculator for official forms or legal deadlines?

It returns the same result as counting completed years, months and days by hand, so it is useful for checking eligibility. For legal, immigration or school-entry decisions, confirm the exact rule and cut-off date with the issuing authority, because some rules count age differently.

How do I find the age difference between two people?

Use the age difference calculator further down this page. Enter both dates of birth and it returns the gap in years, months and days, together with the total number of days between the two dates.

Is my date of birth stored or sent anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and the dates you enter are not sent to or stored on our server.

Why do hours, minutes and seconds differ between age calculators?

Calculators differ in whether they use birth time, time zone, midnight or the current moment. This one counts from midnight at the start of your birth date and, when the chosen date is today, up to the current moment on your device. Treat the hours, minutes and seconds as close estimates.
