Stride Length vs Step Length: What’s the Difference?

People mix up stride length and step length, and many fitness trackers use “stride length” to mean one step. The two are linked by a simple rule: one stride is two steps.

  • Step length: left foot to right foot (one step).
  • Stride length: left foot to the next left foot (two steps).

Key formula: stride length = step length × 2. If your tracker shows 10,000 steps, that is 5,000 strides. Mixing the two up doubles or halves your distance estimate, which also skews anything a calorie calculator works out from distance.

How to Measure Stride Length: 4 Methods

Here are four ways to find your own number, from most to least accurate.

Method 1: Track or measured route (most accurate)

  1. Use a standard 400 m track, or any route you have measured.
  2. Run or walk one lap at your normal pace.
  3. Count every step, or count each time your right foot lands and double it.
  4. Calculate: step length = 400 ÷ steps, and stride = step × 2.
  5. Example: 165 right-foot landings = 330 steps, so step = 400 ÷ 330 = 1.21 m and stride = 2.42 m (7.9 ft).

Method 2: At home, no equipment

  1. Mark a start line on the floor with tape.
  2. Stand behind the line and walk 10 natural steps.
  3. Mark where your 10th step lands and measure from the line to that mark.
  4. Calculate: step = distance ÷ 10, and stride = step × 2.
  5. Example: 281 in ÷ 10 = 28.1 in step, so stride = 56.2 in (4.7 ft). Repeat three times and average for better accuracy.

Method 3: GPS watch or phone app

  • Many running watches and phone apps report step length or stride length.
  • Check whether the figure is per step or per stride, and whether it needs a sensor.
  • Readings can be off by several percent, especially on turns and in poor GPS conditions.

Note: for accurate calorie tracking, calibrate your device with Method 1 or 2.

Method 4: Height-based estimate (quick)

  • Walking: height × 0.413 = step length, then double for stride.
  • Easy running: height × 0.6 = step length.
  • Race-pace running: height × 0.75 = step length.

Example for 5'10" (70 in): walking step = 70 × 0.413 = 28.9 in (2.4 ft), stride = 57.8 in (4.8 ft). Easy-run step = 70 × 0.6 = 42 in (3.5 ft), stride = 84 in (7.0 ft).

Stride to Height Ratio: What Is Normal?

The stride to height ratio compares your step or stride with your height. Two conventions are in use: step ÷ height and stride ÷ height (twice as large). The ratio rises with speed, so the right comparison depends on how fast you are moving.

ActivityStep ÷ heightStride ÷ height
Walking, normal pace≈ 0.41≈ 0.83
Easy jog (9:00–10:00 per mile)0.55–0.621.1–1.2
Recreational race pace (7:00–8:00)0.65–0.751.3–1.5
Fast running (about 6:00)≈ 0.85≈ 1.7
Elite marathon pace≈ 1.1≈ 2.2
Elite sprint≈ 1.25≈ 2.5

How to calculate: step length ÷ height, using the same unit for both. Example: 1.2 m ÷ 1.73 m = 0.69. The ratio alone cannot tell you whether you overstride, because landing position and cadence matter more. Body composition also affects running mechanics, so you may want to check your body fat percentage for context.

Values are approximate and based on a runner of average height; yours will vary. If your result is far outside these ranges, the usual cause is a counting mistake, such as counting only one foot.

Overstriding: The Most Common Stride Mistake

Overstriding means your foot lands well ahead of your body’s center of mass. It tends to add braking and impact on each step. It is about where your foot lands, not simply how long your step is.

The dashed line marks the body’s center of mass.

Signs you may be overstriding

  • Your heel lands well ahead of your hips, with a braking feel or a loud heel strike.
  • Your cadence is low for your pace compared with your own easy runs.
  • Your step is much longer than the height chart predicts for your pace.
  • You get recurring knee, hip or shin soreness. A physiotherapist or sports medicine clinician can assess your form, and body size and training load also affect impact (our BMI calculator gives one point of context).

How to work on it

  1. Raise cadence gradually: try 5–10% higher, using a metronome app.
  2. Land closer to your body: aim to place your foot under your hips rather than reaching forward.
  3. Shorten the reach, not the push: the change should feel small.
  4. Build strength: glutes and calves help control landing.
  5. Try gentle hills: they naturally shorten the step.

What the research says: lab studies of runners, including Heiderscheit et al. (2011), have found that raising step rate by about 5–10% reduces loading at the hip and knee. Results vary by runner, so change gradually and see a clinician if you have pain. Hydration matters on longer runs, so use our water intake calculator to plan fluids.

Track your own trend

  • Measure your step length with Method 2 in the measuring guide above every few months, in the same shoes and on the same surface.
  • Time yourself over a fixed distance as well. Walking speed is often used by clinicians as a general health indicator.
  • Strength and balance training are commonly recommended to help maintain walking ability.

When to talk to a clinician

  • A sudden change in your walking pattern or a marked slowdown.
  • Shuffling, new pain, or falls and near-falls.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average stride length by height?

For walking, a common rule of thumb is step length ≈ 0.413 × height for women and 0.415 × height for men, and stride length is two steps. Someone 5'8" (173 cm) tall has a walking step of about 28.2 in (72 cm) and a stride of about 4.7 ft (143 cm). Running steps are longer: roughly 0.55–0.65 × height at an easy pace and 0.70–0.80 × height at race pace.

What is the average stride length for a woman or a man?

It depends mostly on height. For a woman of about 5'4" (163 cm), the walking step is about 26.5 in (67 cm), the stride about 4.4 ft (135 cm), and she takes roughly 2,390 steps per mile. For a man of about 5'9" (175 cm), the step is about 28.6 in (73 cm), the stride about 4.8 ft (145 cm), and roughly 2,220 steps per mile. See the height chart for other heights.

What is the difference between stride length and step length?

Step length is the distance from one foot to the opposite foot (left heel to right heel). Stride length is the distance from one foot to that same foot’s next landing, so stride = 2 × step. Fitness trackers and many websites use “stride length” to mean a single step, so check which one a number refers to. See the visual diagram.

How do I calculate stride length from steps?

Step length = distance ÷ steps, and stride length = distance ÷ (steps ÷ 2). Example: 100 m covered in 140 steps is 70 strides, so the stride is 100 ÷ 70 = 1.43 m (4.7 ft) and the step is 0.71 m (2.3 ft). If you only count one foot, each count is already a stride.

What is a good stride length for a 5'10" man?

There is no single ideal, but typical values are: walking step about 2.4 ft (74 cm) (stride about 4.8 ft), easy-run step about 3.5 ft (107 cm), and race-pace step about 4.4 ft (133 cm). Compare your own numbers using the ratio table.

What is a normal stride to height ratio?

Walking at a normal pace, step length is about 0.41 × your height (stride about 0.83 × height). Easy running is roughly 0.55–0.62 × height per step and faster running is higher. The ratio rises with speed, so compare it with the row that matches your pace in the full ratio guide. If your result is far outside the range, first re-check your step count.

How many steps in a mile for a 5'6" person?

A 5'6" person has a walking step of about 2.28 ft, which works out to roughly 2,320 steps per mile. Running takes fewer steps because each step is longer: about 1,600 per mile at an easy pace and 1,280 at race pace. See the steps per mile by height chart.

How many steps in a kilometer?

Steps per km = 3,281 ÷ step length in feet. A 5'6" walker (step about 2.28 ft) takes roughly 1,440 steps per km. See the steps in a kilometer chart.

How can I measure my walking stride length at home?

Mark a start line, walk 10 natural steps, mark where the last step lands, and measure the distance. Divide by 10 for step length and double it for stride length. Repeating it three times improves accuracy. The full guide is above.

What is the average stride length for a 5'4" woman?

Walking step about 26.5 in (67 cm), stride about 4.4 ft (135 cm), around 2,390 steps per mile. Easy running step is about 3.2 ft (98 cm).

Does height affect stride length?

Yes, height is the main driver, but leg length, flexibility, strength, speed, terrain and footwear all matter, so two people of the same height can differ by 10% or more. Body composition can play a role too; our BMI calculator is one quick reference point.

Should I focus on stride length or cadence?

Most coaches suggest working on cadence and form rather than consciously lengthening your stride. A common target is 170–180 steps per minute, but your natural cadence depends on pace and height, so treat that as a reference, not a rule. If you experiment, change it gradually (about 5–10%).

Is a longer stride always better for speed?

No. Speed = step length × cadence, so both matter. Reaching forward to lengthen your step can make you land ahead of your body, which adds braking and impact. The fastest runners combine a long step for their height with a high cadence.

How many steps are in 50 meters?

Divide 50 m by your step length. At a normal walking pace a 5'2" person takes about 77 steps and a 5'10" person about 68 steps. The steps-per-mile section above lists steps in 50 m, 100 m and a 400 m track lap for every height.

How many steps in 40 minutes of walking?

It depends on cadence, not height. A normal to brisk walk is often about 100–120 steps per minute, so 40 minutes is roughly 4,000–4,800 steps. At 110 steps per minute a 5'2" walker covers about 1.8 miles (2.9 km) and a 5'10" walker about 2.0 miles (3.2 km).

How far is 10,000 or 12,500 steps?

A 5'2" walker covers about 4.1 miles (6.5 km) in 10,000 steps and 5.1 miles (8.1 km) in 12,500. A 5'10" walker covers 4.6 miles (7.4 km) and 5.7 miles (9.2 km).

Do taller people take fewer steps in a mile?

Yes. Walking, a 5'0" person takes about 2,550 steps per mile and a 6'4" person about 2,010. Fewer steps does not mean faster, though: speed is step length × cadence, so a shorter runner with a higher cadence can match a taller one.

What is the average step length for a child?

In a study of 103 typically developing children aged 5 to 13 (Voss et al., 2020), the average walking step was about 50 cm at ages 5–6 (children about 115 cm tall), 55 cm at 7–8, 60 cm at 9–10 and 66 cm at 11–13. That is about 0.42–0.43 × height. No reliable table was found for children under 5, so measure a toddler's steps directly.

What is the average step length for adults over 70?

In 294 community-dwelling adults aged 70 and over (Hollman et al., 2011), average walking step length ran from about 69 cm (men) and 61 cm (women) at ages 70–74 down to about 59 cm and 54 cm at 85 and over, at roughly 100–115 steps per minute. That is about 2,330–2,730 steps per mile for men and 2,640–2,980 for women, though height and fitness matter too.

What is the step length of a very tall person?

The height rule keeps working at the extremes, but individuals vary more. A 6'7" person has a walking step of about 32.7 in (83 cm, 2.73 ft), and a 7'0" person about 34.8 in (88 cm, 2.90 ft). Measure yours with the 10-step method for a more exact figure.

How These Numbers Are Calculated

  • Walking step length: height × 0.413 (women) or 0.415 (men), a widely used rule of thumb. The tables use 0.414 because the difference is under 0.5%.
  • Running step length: height × 0.55–0.65 at an easy pace and 0.70–0.80 at race pace, estimated from typical speed ÷ cadence. Real values rise with speed and vary by runner.
  • Stride length: two steps.
  • Steps per mile or km: 5,280 ft or 3,280.84 ft divided by step length in feet.
  • Pace table: step length = speed ÷ cadence, using the middle of the cadence range shown.
  • Children and adults over 70: published group averages from the studies listed under Sources, not height-based formulas. Under-5 data is not included.
  • Steps in 50 m, 400 m and by time: distance ÷ walking step length, or cadence × minutes.

These are estimates for general information, not medical advice. If walking or running causes pain, a physiotherapist or sports medicine clinician can assess your gait.
