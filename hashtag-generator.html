What This Tool Does

Enter any topic and get a list of platform-specific hashtags. The tool builds tags from the words you type (for example, home workout becomes #homeworkout, #home, #workout, #homeworkouttips and similar variations) and adds a few general tags for the platform you choose. The result is a pool to choose from, not a set to paste in full: up to 20 tags for Instagram, up to 10 for TikTok and up to 3 for X (Twitter). Pick the few that fit your post.

Click any tag to copy it individually, or use Copy All to grab the full list. All processing happens in your browser — no data is sent to any server.

Platform-Specific Limits

  • Instagram: Instagram announced in December 2025 that it is gradually limiting captions to 5 hashtags per post or reel (it had been testing a limit of 3). The rollout is gradual, so your account may still allow more. Choose your best 5 from the pool. Source: Social Media Today.
  • TikTok: Use 3–5. Industry reports say TikTok now caps captions at 5 hashtags, but I could not find a TikTok announcement, so check the limit in the app when you post.
  • X (Twitter): One or two is a common habit, so the post still reads naturally. This is a style choice, not a platform rule.

Hashtag Strategy by Platform

Instagram

Instagram says more targeted hashtags work better than many generic ones. With a 5-hashtag limit, a simple mix is:

  • 1 broad (1M+ posts): #fitness, #travel, #food — high reach, high competition
  • 2 medium (100K–1M): #fitnessmotivation, #travelphotography — balanced reach
  • 2 niche (under 100K): #homeworkouttips, #solotravelasia — targeted audiences

The limit applies to the caption. Use Instagram's search to check a hashtag's post count before posting. The split above is a rule of thumb, not an Instagram rule.

TikTok

On TikTok, a hashtag helps describe what the video is about. A simple mix is:

  • 1 broad hashtag (#fyp, #viral, #trending)
  • 1–2 niche hashtags specific to your content
  • 1 branded or community hashtag if applicable

Twitter

X (Twitter) posts read best when hashtags are part of the conversation. Try to keep them:

  • Specific to the conversation (e.g., #WWDC2026, not just #tech)
  • Used in sentences, not appended at the end
  • Limited to 1–2 per tweet to avoid looking automated

When to Use This Tool

📸 Instagram Posts

Planning a post? Generate the pool, then narrow it to the 5 most relevant. Check each hashtag's post count in Instagram search to see that it is active.

🎬 TikTok Videos

Before uploading, generate the pool and pick the 3–5 most relevant to your video's theme. Tags like #fyp say nothing about the content, so add specific ones too.

🐦 Twitter Threads

Use 1–2 event-specific or topic hashtags in your thread opener. Don't hashtag every tweet in a thread — it looks spammy.

📊 Content Calendars

Batch-generate hashtags for upcoming posts. Save platform-specific lists in a spreadsheet for quick copy-paste during scheduling.

Hashtag Research Tips

  • Check volume before using: A hashtag with only a few posts reaches few people; one with hundreds of millions is crowded. Many creators aim for the middle, but there is no official number.
  • Monitor competitors: Check which hashtags successful accounts in your niche use regularly.
  • Match each post: Choose tags for each post instead of pasting the same set every time.
  • Keep tags honest: Hashtags that do not describe the post (for example #food on a fitness post) mislead viewers and can break platform rules.
  • Test and track: Note which tags you used and compare reach in each platform's post analytics.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hashtags should I use per platform?

Instagram: Instagram announced in December 2025 that it is gradually limiting captions to 5 hashtags per post or reel, so choose your best 5. TikTok: 3-5 is a common choice, and reports say TikTok caps captions at 5, so check the limit in the app. X (Twitter): one or two keeps the post readable; this is a style habit, not a rule.

Does this tool use real trending data?

No. The tags are built from your keyword plus a few general tags, and the tool does not know how many posts each tag has or whether it is trending. Search each tag in the app to check that it is active and not restricted before you use it.

Should I use popular or niche hashtags?

Use both. Popular hashtags (1M+ posts) reach more people but have more competition. Niche hashtags (under 100K posts) have less competition and reach a more targeted audience. With only 5 slots on Instagram, favor specific tags and add at most one broad one.

Can banned hashtags hurt my account?

Yes. Some hashtags are banned or restricted by platforms due to spam or policy violations. Platforms can restrict banned tags, and using them can limit how far your post is shown. Always check a hashtag's search page before using it — if it shows a warning or no recent posts, avoid it.

How the tags are built (with an example)

The tool does not look up trends or post counts. It uses a fixed recipe on the words you type:

  1. It lowercases your text, removes punctuation and keeps the first 4 words.
  2. It joins the words into one tag (home workout becomes #homeworkout).
  3. It adds one tag for each word of 3 or more letters (#home, #workout).
  4. For Instagram it adds the joined tag plus a suffix such as tips, ideas, daily or goals, then 5 general tags (#instagood, #explore, #reels, #instadaily, #photooftheday). For TikTok it adds tips, tok, hacks and tutorial endings, then #fyp, #foryou and #viral. For X it keeps only the first 3 tags.

Example, "home workout" on TikTok gives 10 tags: #homeworkout #home #workout #homeworkouttips #homeworkouttok #homeworkouthacks #homeworkouttutorial #fyp #foryou #viral. With the 5-tag limit you might keep #homeworkout, #homeworkouttips, #workout, #homeworkouthacks and #fyp.

Same input on X (Twitter) gives 3 tags: #homeworkout #home #workout. A one-letter or two-letter word such as "a" is skipped in step 3, so "a b" gives only #ab.
