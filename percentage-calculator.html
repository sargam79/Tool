Quick answer: a percentage is a number out of 100. To find X% of Y, multiply Y by X and divide by 100. To find what percent A is of B, divide A by B and multiply by 100. For a change, divide the difference by the original value and multiply by 100.

Quick answers to common percentage questions

What is 20% of 150?

30. Multiply 150 by 0.20.

45 is what percent of 180?

25%. Divide 45 by 180 and multiply by 100.

What is the percent change from 80 to 100?

25% increase. (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100.

What is 15% off 250?

212.5. You save 37.5.

What is 85 plus 8% tax?

91.8. Multiply 85 by 1.08.

A 50% rise then a 50% fall: where do you end up?

75 from a start of 100, so a net 25% loss.

What is an 18% tip on 80 with 8% tax, split two ways?

50.4 each. Tip 14.4 + tax 6.4 + bill 80 = 100.8.

What is 20% of 50%?

10%. Multiply 20 by 50 and divide by 100.

Percentage formulas at a glance

  • Percent of a number: (Percentage × Number) ÷ 100. Example: 20% of 150 = (20 × 150) ÷ 100 = 30.
  • What percent X is of Y: (X ÷ Y) × 100. Example: 45 of 180 = 25%.
  • Percentage change: ((New − Original) ÷ Original) × 100. Example: 80 to 100 = 25% increase; 100 to 80 = 20% decrease.
  • Percentage difference: |A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100. Example: 50 and 70 = 33.3333%.
  • Add a percentage: Number × (1 + P ÷ 100). Example: 85 plus 8% = 91.8.
  • Subtract a percentage: Number × (1 − P ÷ 100). Example: 250 less 15% = 212.5.
  • Find the whole: X ÷ (P ÷ 100). Example: 45 is 20% of 225.
  • Original price: Sale price ÷ (1 − Discount ÷ 100). Example: 68 after 15% off was 80.
  • Successive changes: Start × (1 + P1 ÷ 100) × (1 + P2 ÷ 100). Example: 100, then +50%, then −50% = 75.
  • Percentage points: To − From. Example: 4% to 6% = 2 points, or a 50% relative increase.
  • Bill with tax and tip: Bill × (1 + Tax ÷ 100) + Bill × Tip ÷ 100. Example: 80 with 8% tax and 18% tip = 100.8.

Mental math shortcuts

  • 10%: move the decimal point one place left. 10% of 250 = 25.
  • 1%: move the decimal point two places left. 1% of 250 = 2.5.
  • 5%: half of 10%. 5% of 250 = 12.5.
  • 15% (a common tip): 10% plus 5%. 15% of 80 = 8 + 4 = 12.
  • 20%: double 10%, or divide by 5. 20% of 80 = 16.
  • 25%: divide by 4. 25% of 200 = 50.
  • 50%: divide by 2.

Common percentage mistakes

  • Mixing up the base. A 25% increase from 80 to 100 is not undone by a 25% decrease. Going from 100 back to 80 is a 20% decrease, because the base is now 100.
  • Adding discounts. 20% off followed by another 10% off is not 30% off. 100 becomes 80, then 72, which is 28% off in total.
  • Confusing percent with percentage points. Moving from 4% to 6% is 2 percentage points, but a 50% increase in the rate.
  • Reversing a discount by adding it back. If 15% off gives 68, the original was 80, not 78.2. Use the original price calculator above.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for a percentage?

To find a percentage of a number, multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100: (Percentage × Number) ÷ 100. To find what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. For example, 45 is what percent of 180? 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25, and 0.25 × 100 = 25%.

How do I calculate percentage increase or decrease?

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original value, and multiply by 100. A positive answer is an increase and a negative answer is a decrease. Going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase, and going from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?

Percentage change measures movement from an original value to a new one, so the order matters. Percentage difference compares two numbers without treating either as the starting point, by dividing their gap by their average, so the order does not matter. For 50 and 70, the change from 50 to 70 is 40%, while the percentage difference is 33.33%.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the sale price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal. If an item costs 68 after 15% off, the original price is 68 ÷ 0.85 = 80. Do not just add 15% back to 68, because the discount was taken from the larger original price.

How do I add a percentage, like sales tax, to a number?

Multiply the number by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. Adding 8% tax to 85 gives 85 × 1.08 = 91.80. To subtract a percentage, multiply by 1 minus the decimal instead, so 250 less 15% is 250 × 0.85 = 212.50.

Why does a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease not return to the start?

Because the second percentage is taken from a different, larger base. Starting at 100, a 50% increase gives 150, and a 50% decrease of 150 is 75, not 100. To undo a 50% increase you need a decrease of about 33.3%.

How do successive percentage changes work?

Multiply the starting value by one plus each change as a decimal, in order. Starting at 100, a 50% increase and then a 50% decrease gives 100 × 1.5 × 0.5 = 75, a net loss of 25%. Successive changes cannot simply be added together.

How do I calculate a tip and split the bill?

Work out the tax and tip on the bill before tax, add both to the bill, then divide by the number of people. An 80 bill with 8% tax and an 18% tip is 80 + 6.40 + 14.40 = 100.80, which is 50.40 each for two people.

What is the difference between a percentage and a percentage point?

A percentage point is the plain arithmetic gap between two percentages. If an interest rate moves from 4% to 6%, it rose by 2 percentage points, which is a 50% increase in the rate.
